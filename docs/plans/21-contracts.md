# 21 — Contracts: distance sales and other agreements for the seller's customers

## The request
Owner, 2026-11-01: add a contracts section to the plans so a seller (a Payrudder tenant) can present a **distance sales contract** ("mesafeli satış") or other agreements to *their* customers.

## Where we are
Nothing exists for a seller's customers (searched the gateway, portal, checkout, payment links, customer and settings code). What exists is adjacent:
- The tenant owner accepts **Payrudder's own** terms at go-live: `tenants.terms_accepted_at/_by` (`services/plans-svc/migrations/00001_init.sql`, `golive/command`). It is a timestamp and a user id with no text version, hash, IP or user agent, so it is the wrong shape for customer evidence. Do not reuse it.
- Seller legal data exists (`LegalInfo`: legal name, tax id, tax office, MERSIS, trade registry, address in `resources.proto`, edited on the e-invoice page).
- Customer language and kind (individual / business) exist (`CustomerLocales` = en, tr, de, fr, es, ar). Hosted pages render in the customer's language (`hosted*_i18n.go`, `customerLang`).
- Hosted pages have no terms link or checkbox; the only privacy text covers the referral cookie.
- The gateway reads the client IP (`hosted.go`, trusted `X-Forwarded-For` helper in `session.go`); the user agent is not captured.
- `assets-svc` stores binaries; `notifier` sends customer mails in six languages; webhook event names live in `plans-svc/internal/webhooks/model`.

### The follow-ups (third PR, `feat/contracts-followups`)

Built, each with the test that fails without it:

- **A withdrawal routes to a refund, only after the seller decided.** Accepting a withdrawal that names an invoice, with the new setting `withdrawal_refund` = `full` (the default; `none` leaves the refund to the seller), asks billing for the refund through the credit-note path billing already has: `BillingAdminService.CreateCreditNote` with `refund_to_card` for `refund_amount_minor` (0 is whatever is left of the invoice). Billing keeps its own rules: a paid invoice only, never more than what is left of it, only a payment a provider can return. The gateway makes the call (the same elevation `sandbox.go` uses; no tenant key can create a credit note itself) and **writes the answer on the withdrawal**: `credit_note_id`, `refund_state` (`none`, `requesting`, `requested`, `outside`, `failed`) and `refund_note`. A card payment is `requested`; an invoice paid by bank transfer or cash is refused by billing with `nothing_to_refund` and the withdrawal is marked `outside` ("refund outside Payrudder": the seller refunds it by hand and then marks the withdrawal refunded); a failure is `failed` and can be asked again with `POST /v1/contract-withdrawals/{id}/refund` (also the way to ask when the setting is `none`). The decision is always recorded first and the call never fails because of the refund. **Never twice:** plans-svc hands the request to one caller (`ClaimContractWithdrawalRefund`, a row lock and a state), a request left standing five minutes by a caller that died is taken over, and the new caller first looks through the invoice's credit notes for the one whose reason is `withdrawal <id>` before making another. The webhook `contract.withdrawal_decided` is new (`credit_note.created` already announces the credit note). Tests: gateway `TestAcceptingAWithdrawal...`, `TestTheSettingNone...`, `TestAWithdrawalPaidOutside...`, `TestARefundIsNeverAskedForTwice...` (each broken once on purpose: the gate on the setting failed `TestTheSettingNone`); plans-svc `ContractFollowups` ("a refund is claimed by one caller...").
- **The renewal notice.** `internal/contracts/renewals.go` (`Renewer`, a job like the trial reminder, with cells): for a subscription Payrudder runs whose fixed fee is invoiced in advance (`fee_due_at` is the renewal moment), whose customer has an address and accepted an agreement, `renewal_notice_days` before it (default 7, 0 switches it off, at most 60, per seller) the customer is mailed once, in their language, the price of the next period (after the subscription's coupon), the date in the seller's time zone and the seller's own words on how to cancel (`renewal_cancel_texts`, one per language, plain text). Idempotency is the table `contract_renewal_notices` (primary key tenant, customer, `renews_at`, inserted in the transaction that writes the fact), which is also the stored copy of what was sent; the fact is `RenewalNotice` on `subscription.renewal-notice.v1`; the notifier mail is `renewal_notice.v1` in six languages. A subscription billed in arrears has no renewal moment and a usage-dependent price, a provider's subscription is the provider's to announce, a trialing one has the trial reminder: none of them is noticed. Tests: plans-svc `ContractFollowups` ("the renewal notice: once per renewal..."), notifier unit tests of the mail data and of every language.
- **The portal.** `/c/{token}` lists what the customer accepted (newest version of each kind, with a link to the stored page, served from `/c/{token}/agreements/{id}` under a policy that runs nothing, only for the portal customer's own acceptances), asks them to accept again when a material version was published since (only a customer who accepted something before: a first acceptance happens where they pay; renewals are still never held back) with `POST /c/{token}/agreements` (acceptance source `hosted`, new context kind `portal`, the address and browser the server saw), and links to the withdrawal function. Tests: gateway `TestThePortal...`.
- **The online withdrawal function (Directive (EU) 2023/2673 art. 11a).** Read on 2026-11-02 from the Publications Office text of the directive, article 1(3): the trader of a distance contract concluded through an online interface ensures that the consumer can withdraw with **a withdrawal function labelled "withdraw from contract here"** or an unambiguous corresponding formulation, easily legible, **continuously available throughout the withdrawal period, prominently displayed and easily accessible (1)**; the function lets the consumer send an online statement in which they **easily provide or confirm their name, details identifying the contract and details of the electronic means by which the confirmation will be sent (2)**; once they completed it, a **confirmation function labelled in an easily legible manner, and only with the words "confirm withdrawal"** or an unambiguous corresponding formulation (3); once they activate it the trader sends **an acknowledgement of receipt on a durable medium, including its content and the date and time of its submission, without undue delay (4)**; a statement submitted before the period expired is in time (5). ### The PDF copies (fifth PR, `feat/product-tail-3-agreements`, 2026-11-02)

Built, each with the test that fails without it:

- **The PDF is drawn from the stored page, on request, and never stored.** `internal/contracts/snapshotpdf` reads the HTML page of an acceptance's group (the one whose sha256 the acceptance holds; the hash is checked first, as for the page itself) and draws it as text: the headings, the fields, the lists, the words next to the boxes, the hashes, and at the end the sha256 of the stored page the PDF came from. The same page and acceptance time give the same bytes (`SetCreationDate` is the time of the acceptance). `GetContractSnapshotRequest.include_pdf` asks for it; the response carries `pdf` or `pdf_unavailable`. **Font:** the platform's one PDF font, Noto Sans, now in `pkg/pdffont` (moved out of the invoice worker; the invoice worker imports it, nothing about invoices changed). **Default (not agreed): a page with a character the font cannot draw (every Arabic snapshot, a customer named in Chinese) gets no PDF** (`pdf_unavailable`, the HTML page remains the only copy) rather than a PDF with question marks; Noto Sans has no Arabic and the drawing is left to right.
- **The mail carries it.** The notifier asks for the PDF with the snapshot, attaches it as `agreement-<first 12 characters of the hash>.pdf` (`mailx.Message.Attachments`: SMTP sends MIME parts, Resend its `attachments` array of filename and base64 content, read from its API reference on 2026-11-02; the file name goes through `mime.FormatMediaType`, so a name cannot add a header) and adds one line, in the six languages, saying a PDF copy is attached. The page stays in the body: a mail without the PDF is still the customer's copy. **Nafru:** it takes Resend's shape, but that it accepts `attachments` is UNVERIFIED (Nafru's contract is still not known); if it drops the field the customer has the page in the body.
- **The portal** lists "PDF copy" beside "View what you accepted" (`GET /c/{token}/agreements/{id}/pdf`, a download; another customer's acceptance or an unknown portal is the usual 404; no link is offered for an acceptance served in Arabic, and one that has none shows a short page in the customer's language).

## Legal basis (this is **not** legal advice)

Read on 2026-11-02 from the official texts. What was read is quoted by article number; what was not is marked UNVERIFIED.

### Turkey: Mesafeli Sözleşmeler Yönetmeliği (consolidated text)

- Source: mevzuat.gov.tr, consolidated PDF of the regulation (mevzuat no. 20237), <https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/MevzuatMetin/yonetmelik/7.5.20237.pdf>, fetched 2026-11-02 (the PDF's metadata says it was produced on 30 July 2026). Cross-checked against the Resmî Gazete original, <https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2014/11/20141127-6.htm> (RG 27.11.2014, no. 29188), fetched 2026-11-02. Amendments in the consolidated text: RG 9.2.2019 (30681), 23.8.2022 (31932), 4.11.2023 (32359), 10.8.2024 (32628), 24.5.2025 (32909). The 2025 amendment's provisions apply from 1.1.2026. Items (ı), (j), (k) of art. 15(1) are marked annulled by Danıştay 10. Daire (6.5.2026, E.2022/5534, K.2026/2753); they are about vehicles, live auctions and installed goods and do not touch this feature.
- **Art. 5(1)** lists the pre-information (a) to (k): main characteristics; seller and intermediary name, MERSIS or tax number; contact and complaint details; total price with all taxes and, for a subscription or an indefinite contract, **per billing period (5(4))**; payment and performance; **(g) the withdrawal conditions, period, procedure**; (ğ) where to send the notice; **(h) that the right is not available or when it is lost under art. 15**; (k) consumer arbitration committee and court. **5(2)**: it is an integral part of the contract and cannot be changed unless both sides expressly agree. **5(6)**: the seller (and an intermediary) must **prove** the pre-information was given.
- **Art. 6(1)**: in writing or on a **durable medium** (defined in 4(c): e-mail and the like), at least 12 point, plain language. **6(2)(a)**: for an internet contract, items (a), (d), (g), (h) of art. 5 are **shown as a whole, clearly, immediately before the consumer enters the payment obligation**; 6(2)(b): shipping limits and accepted payment methods at the latest before the order.
- **Art. 7**: the seller must have the consumer **confirm** having received the pre-information in a way that fits the medium; **otherwise the contract is not concluded**. **Art. 8(1)**: before the consumer confirms the order, it must be stated clearly that the order means a payment obligation; **otherwise the consumer is not bound by the order**.
- **Art. 9(1)-(2)**: 14 days without reason or penalty; for a service the period starts **on the day the contract is concluded**. **Art. 10(1)**: if the consumer was not properly informed of the right, the 14 days do not bind them and the right ends one year after the normal period. **Art. 11(1)-(2)**: the notice may be given in writing or on a durable medium; if the seller offers a form or statement **on its website, it must confirm receipt to the consumer immediately**. **Art. 12(3)**: for a service, the refund is due within 14 days of the notice.
- **Art. 15(1)** (opening words: "unless the parties agreed otherwise"): **(ğ)** services performed instantly in electronic form and intangible goods delivered instantly; **(h)** services whose performance **starts with the consumer's approval before the withdrawal period ends**. The regulation does not say in what form the approval is given or recorded: UNVERIFIED how the Ministry or the courts read it; the product records it as its own box with its words.
- **Art. 19**: an extra charge needs the consumer's explicit approval; options that are pre-selected must not be, and what was paid because of them is refunded at once. (The boxes are unticked.)
- **Art. 20(1)**: the seller keeps the information and documents of each transaction on withdrawal, information, delivery and the other duties **for three years**; **20(2)** the same for an intermediary service provider. This is the source of the default retention of 36 months. How long the **IP address** (personal data) may be kept beyond the evidence duty under KVKK is UNVERIFIED.
- **Intermediary**: art. 4(i)-(j) defines an *aracı hizmet sağlayıcı* as the person who, with a system built for it, mediates the conclusion of distance contracts on behalf of a seller; arts. 5(7), 6(6), 12/A(3), 12/A(5) make it jointly responsible for the pre-information, its confirmation and its proof, and for keeping the records for three years, and 12/A(1) obliges it to run a system for notices of withdrawal on the platform. **UNVERIFIED and for the owner and a lawyer: whether Payrudder's hosted pages make it an intermediary.** If it does, keeping the evidence itself (as built) is part of the duty, and the withdrawal system of art. 12/A is not yet built.
- Not read: Law 6502 itself (art. 48 is the regulation's basis), KVKK.

### EU: Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU

- Source: the Publications Office, which publishes EUR-Lex (eur-lex.europa.eu answers scripted requests with an empty page): the OJ text, <https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32011L0083> (OJ L 304, 22.11.2011); the consolidated text of 28.05.2022, <https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/02011L0083-20220528> (after Directive (EU) 2019/2161); and Directive (EU) 2023/2673, <https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32023L2673> (OJ 28.11.2023). All fetched 2026-11-02. A later consolidation was **not retrievable** (the dated identifiers I tried returned 404): UNVERIFIED whether another amendment changed these articles after 28.05.2022.
- **Art. 6(1)** lists the information before the consumer is bound, including **(h)** the conditions, time limit and procedure of the right of withdrawal with the model form of Annex I(B), **(k)** that there is no right where art. 16 applies and when it is lost, **(o)** the duration and, for an automatically extended contract, the conditions for ending it. **6(9)**: the trader bears the burden of proof. **Art. 8(1)**: plain language, legible on a durable medium.
- **Art. 8(2)**: for an electronic order with an obligation to pay, the trader makes the consumer aware, clearly and prominently and **directly before the order**, of points (a), (e), (o), (p); the consumer must **explicitly acknowledge** the obligation; a button is labelled **only** "order with obligation to pay" or an unambiguous equivalent; **otherwise the consumer is not bound**. (The pay button says exactly this in six languages.) **Art. 8(7)**: confirmation of the contract **on a durable medium** (the mail), with the art. 6(1) information unless given before on a durable medium, and (b) the confirmation of the consent and acknowledgement of art. 16(m). **Art. 8(8)** (2022 text): to start a service during the withdrawal period the trader requires an **express request** and asks the consumer to **acknowledge that, once the contract has been fully performed, the right of withdrawal is gone**: these are the two boxes `consent_start` and `consent_waiver`.
- **Art. 9(1)-(2)**: 14 days; for a service from the conclusion of the contract. **Art. 14(3)**: a consumer who withdraws after such a request pays an amount in proportion to what was provided. **Art. 16(a)**: no right of withdrawal after a service has been **fully performed**, if the performance began with the consumer's **prior express consent and acknowledgement** that the right is lost. **Art. 16(m)**: digital content not on a tangible medium, when the consumer gave prior express consent, acknowledged the loss and the trader sent the art. 8(7) confirmation.
- **Directive (EU) 2023/2673 art. 1(3)** inserts **art. 11a** in 2011/83 (text read in full on 2026-11-02 from the Publications Office, <https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32023L2673>, fetched with `Accept: application/xhtml+xml`): see "The follow-ups" for the five paragraphs. Article 1(2) also changes art. 6(1)(h): the information given before the order includes, where applicable, **the existence and placement of the withdrawal function**. Member States apply it **from 19 June 2026** (transposition by 19 December 2025). UNVERIFIED: the national transposition of any Member State, and whether the starter texts must now name the function (the seller's text is the seller's; the starter pre-information does not mention it yet). **Built** in the third PR.
- EU-wide auto-renewal rules: none found; national rules differ. **UNVERIFIED.**

## Decisions: the defaults taken (not agreed, the owner may overturn them)
- **Kinds that ship first**: distance sales, pre-information, terms of service, privacy notice. Later: DPA, refund policy, auto-renewal terms.
- **Template text**: structure and variables for every language, **starter text for Turkish and English only**, each beginning with the notice "TEMPLATE - not legal advice. Have a lawyer review and adapt this text before you use it." (Turkish: "ŞABLON - hukuki tavsiye değildir. ..."). The sentences next to the boxes and on the button are the product's wording in six languages; the German, French, Spanish and Arabic ones are UNVERIFIED by a lawyer.
- **Retention**: configurable 12 to 120 months, default **36** for the IP address and user agent (TR art. 20 gives three years for the transaction records; the personal-data side is UNVERIFIED); nothing else is erased; the seller is the controller, the erasure is Payrudder's job on the seller's setting.
- **Who decides a withdrawal**: the seller. Payrudder records the statement, the decision and who made it, and sends the webhooks; the refund follows the decision (third PR, above): billing is asked only after the seller accepted, never from the statement.
- **A series has no template row**: the series of (kind, audience) is its versions, the highest number is current, so there is no mutable "current" pointer. `contract_templates` of the proposal is not a table.
- **Acceptance is per customer and version** (not per order): a customer who accepted the current version (or a later minor one) is not asked again; whether the pre-information must be accepted afresh for every order is UNVERIFIED (TR art. 6(2)(a) shows the price "just before" the obligation; the page does show the order's price in the text through the variables, and the box is what is not repeated).
- **Required means published**: a kind can be required only once it has a published version, and a hosted page whose seller requires a kind with none refuses to take a payment rather than skip it.
- **Business customers** get the business series when published, else the consumer text; **unknown counts as a consumer**.
- **The two consents** (`consent_start`, `consent_waiver`) are required, not optional, when `immediate_start` is on: a consumer who will not ask for the service to start now does not buy it on that page.
