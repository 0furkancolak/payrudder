# ADR 0024 — Payrudder sells itself, with its own engine

- Status: Accepted (phase 55). Builds on [ADR 0004](0004-shared-schema-rls-tenancy.md) (tenants and RLS), [ADR 0006](0006-eventual-consistency-for-limits.md) (the counters are eventually consistent), [ADR 0019](0019-license.md) (its owner may sell it as a service) and [ADR 0023](0023-live-and-sandbox-guards.md) (environments).
- Date: 2026-10-27

## Context

The owner of Payrudder may sell it as a service (ADR 0019). A service has plans: Free, Pro, Scale, with limits on events, apps, members and API keys, a price, a trial, invoices, and a way to upgrade. The product already contains everything such a thing needs: plans with prices and entitlements, customers, subscriptions, a quota engine with hard, soft and notify modes, metering, invoices, payment links and a customer portal. Building a second system beside it ("billing for Payrudder") would duplicate all of that and would never be exercised as hard as the real one.

## Decision

1. **Dogfood.** One account of the deployment is the **platform account**; its tenant, the **platform tenant**, owns Payrudder's own plans. They are ordinary packages with ordinary versions and entitlements, edited with the package pages of the console (the operator opens the platform tenant by id; it is left out of the operator's tenant list, not out of billing's walk over tenants). Every other account is a **customer** of the platform tenant (`external_id` is the account id) with a **subscription**: Free by default, a trial for the first move to a paid plan, `trialing` → `active`/`past_due` by the same lifecycle as any customer's. Invoices, payment links, the portal and dunning are the existing ones.
2. **A plan's limits are its entitlements.** Four meters of the platform tenant carry them: `events` (SUM; counted by limits-svc, a calendar month), and `apps`, `members`, `api_keys` (COUNT; counted where the resource is created). The limit of an organisation is the entitlement of the plan version it is on, **replaced** by its own override (`customer_entitlement_overrides`, the operator's manual override) on that meter. A plan can be edited by publishing a new version; organisations that move to the plan afterwards get it.
3. **Enforcement where the resource is created.** Apps, invitations (a seat is held from the invitation) and API keys are checked in plans-svc inside the transaction that creates them, **under the account's advisory lock** (the one that already serialises member changes), so two requests cannot both take the last place. Only a `hard` limit refuses (`plans.saas.quota.apps|members|api_keys`, `429`); `soft` and `notify` are the quota engine's business. Nothing is ever deleted when a limit drops: an organisation over a lower limit can use what it has and cannot add.
4. **Ingest volume is metered into the platform tenant.** For a **live** tenant of an organisation the gateway (a) refuses a batch while the organisation's `events` entitlement is exhausted and hard (`429 gateway.saas.events_quota`), from limits-svc's `CheckEntitlement` of the organisation's customer row, cached for a few seconds; and (b) after ingest accepted a batch, ingests **one event** into the platform tenant for the organisation's customer: `meter events`, quantity = the number of accepted events, idempotency key `saas-events:<first accepted event id>` (a retried batch's events are duplicates and are not counted; a replayed metering is the same key). Sandbox events and the platform's own events are neither limited nor metered. Both steps fail **open** (plans-svc, limits-svc or ingest-svc down does not take ingest down); a lost metering lets an organisation slip a little past its limit, and one batch (at most 500 events) may overshoot a limit it starts under. This is ADR 0006 applied to money-free quotas.
5. **Every account becomes a customer, however it was made.** Accounts are made by sign-up, by passwordless and Google sign-in, by a person for another organisation and by the operator. Rather than hook each, plans-svc makes an account a customer lazily (the plan page, the ingest policy, a plan change) and a **sweep** every minute finds accounts with no subscription in force (including those whose paid plan was cancelled: they fall back to Free) and gives them Free. `Ensure` is idempotent and safe under concurrency: the customer's external id is unique and a customer has one subscription at a time (an exclusion constraint). Without a subscription in force an organisation lives by the latest Free.
6. **Plan changes end one subscription and start the next.** A self-serve move checks that the usage fits the target plan's hard limits (`plans.saas.downgrade_blocked`), refuses "the plan it is on" (`plans.saas.same_plan`, so a double click is not a second subscription), ends the subscription now (a fee paid in advance is credited to the customer's balance) and starts the new one, with the trial if the organisation never had one. The two steps are each atomic and ordered to be safe to stop between (no subscription reads as Free; a failed start puts the old plan back). The operator's override skips the trial and the usage check.
7. **Tenancy.** The platform tenant's rows are under forced RLS like any tenant's; an ordinary tenant sees none of them (tested). plans-svc reads them on the system pool with the platform tenant named in every statement, and writes them only through the `PlansService` handlers acting as that tenant. The organisation's side of the facts (accounts, members, apps, keys) is not tenant data and is read on the system pool, as the identity module does. A person reaches only their own organisation's plan (membership checked in SQL); the operator routes need the operator.
8. **A switch.** `PAYRUDDER_SAAS` (plans-svc and gateway; `off` by default) turns all of it off: no platform tenant is made, no limit applies, the plan calls answer `enabled: false` or `plans.saas.disabled`, the console hides the page. `make setup` and `make dev` set it `on`, with the platform tenant in the sandbox environment (mock provider) so the whole flow works on a laptop; a deployment of its own chooses `live`. The environment and currency of the platform tenant are chosen when it is created and cannot change (a tenant's settings are immutable).

## Consequences

- The platform's pricing is data, not code: a new plan, a price change or a new limit is a package edit. The three seeded plans are only the first version.
- The same quota engine, billing runs, payment links and dunning serve customers and the platform; a bug there shows in both.
- The platform tenant is a single point of metering: its ingest path is the same as everyone's.
- Free organisations get a zero-total invoice each month; the plan page leaves invoices of nothing out.
- Not built: yearly plans for the platform, per-seat pricing, enforcing the events limit for sandboxes, a hard stop of an organisation whose paid plan lapses (it falls back to Free through the lifecycle and the sweep).
